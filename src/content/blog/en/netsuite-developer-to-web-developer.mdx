I worked as a NetSuite developer at Kavak from December 2018 to December 2022, a period that included [the company becoming Mexico’s first unicorn](https://news-room.kavak.com/kavak-celebrates-its-10th-anniversary-from-mexicos-first-unicorn-to-a-comprehensive-platform-for-the-automotive-sector).

My work covered SuiteScript, integrations, and the roles and permissions people needed to do their jobs. Later I reviewed code and coordinated the development team’s work. By the time I returned to full-stack web development, I was thinking differently about how a team makes changes to software that people use every day.

## Plan how to undo a change

A change can pass a code review and still cause trouble for the people who need it. The questions I took from Kavak were simple: What should keep working after this ships? How do we check it? What do we do if it fails?

A rollback depends on what the change touched. Reverting a script can stop it from running again. If it already changed records, those records need their own recovery plan. That distinction matters just as much in a web app as it does in an ERP.

## Leave decisions where the team can find them

We used Jira and Slack to keep work visible. A ticket can hold what was agreed, why it matters, and how someone can check the change. Slack works for the conversation that gets you to a decision. Putting the outcome back in the ticket gives the next person somewhere to pick up the work.

## Ask what the user is trying to finish

I also learned how much context gets lost when developers and users describe the same problem. A developer may ask what the integration returned. The user wants to know whether they can finish the task they opened NetSuite to do.

I try to ask what they were doing, what they expected to happen, and where they had to stop. Those answers give me something I can test. They also help me explain what changed in terms the person can use.
